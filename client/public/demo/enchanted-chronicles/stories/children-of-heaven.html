Story #5

Children of Heaven

Marko, a nine‑year‑old boy with bright eyes and yellow hair, lived in a small, cozy house with his family. He loved simple joys—playing soccer with friends, exploring parks, climbing trees, and racing through narrow streets during hide‑and‑seek. His laughter echoed through the neighborhood, filling it with life.

Ana, an eight‑year‑old girl with golden hair and a radiant smile, lived next door with her mother and grandmother. She adored drawing and painting, spending hours creating colorful pictures of everything around her. Ana and Marko were inseparable—best friends who spent afternoons in the garden or shared stories beneath the old oak tree.

Despite the looming war, their neighborhood remained a pocket of innocence. Their families were close, often sharing meals and celebrations. Marko’s younger sister adored Ana’s drawings, and Ana’s grandmother watched over them lovingly, telling stories from her own childhood.

But as the war crept closer, life began to change. The distant rumble of explosions and the roar of planes overhead became more frequent. Marko’s father warned him to stay away from windows. Ana’s mother comforted her with stories, trying to mask her fear.

One fateful day, the sirens wailed louder than ever. Smoke darkened the sky. Both families rushed to take shelter as the bombing began. The ground shook violently.

And then—everything went dark.

The Heavenly Realm

Marko and Ana opened their eyes in a serene, breathtaking place. Lush green grass stretched endlessly, flowers perfumed the air, and the sky shone a perfect blue. They felt peace unlike anything they had ever known.

“Where are we?” Ana whispered.

“I don’t know,” Marko replied softly. “But… it feels safe.”

Animals gathered around them—deer, rabbits, birds, even wild creatures—calm and gentle, drawn to the children’s presence.

An old man with a long white beard approached, his smile warm and kind. It was Abraham, the biblical patriarch.

“Welcome, children,” he said gently. “How did you come to be here?”

Marko hesitated. “We… don’t know. Everything went dark, and then we were here.”

Abraham nodded. “You are in heaven now—a place of peace, beauty, and safety. You are free from fear.”

The realization washed over them slowly. They were no longer on Earth. But they were together, and they were safe.

A deer stepped forward. “What was your life like on Earth?”

Marko spoke first. “I lived with my mom, dad, and little sister. I loved soccer and exploring parks. But when the war started, everything changed. We were always careful.”

Ana added softly, “I lived with my mom and grandma. I loved drawing. They tried to keep me calm when the sirens wailed… but I could see how scared they were.”

Abraham listened with tears in his eyes—moved by their innocence and bravery.

“You have endured much,” he said. “But here, you will find peace.”

The Wonders of Heaven

Abraham guided them through heaven’s beauty—gardens glowing with vibrant flowers, lakes shimmering like crystal, trees whispering melodies of harmony.

Birds of every color sang overhead. Butterflies danced in the air. Animals followed them lovingly.

“In heaven,” Abraham said, “everything exists in perfect harmony.”

A wise owl asked, “What do you miss most from Earth?”

Marko smiled sadly. “Playing soccer with my friends… and my little sister’s laughter.”

Ana whispered, “Drawing. Painting. And the stories my mom and grandma told me.”

Abraham placed a gentle hand on their shoulders. “You will create new memories here—beautiful ones.”

They visited the Tree of Memories, its trunk carved with stories of souls who found peace in heaven.

“You will add your own memories someday,” Abraham said.

They waded through a crystal river, greeted animals on the opposite bank, and explored meadows filled with glowing flowers.

Marko and Ana felt joy blooming inside them—pure, peaceful, unburdened joy.

Back on Earth

Meanwhile, Earth was drowning in devastation.

Marko’s parents searched through rubble, calling his name. His mother’s cries echoed through the ruins.

His father lifted a slab of debris—and found Marko’s lifeless body beneath it. Their hearts shattered.

Nearby, Ana’s grandmother found Ana’s small body under a fallen stone. She collapsed, cradling her granddaughter in her arms.

Neighbors gathered, helping the grieving families prepare the children for burial. The community mourned deeply.

At the funeral, the small coffins were covered in flowers. Marko’s father spoke through tears: “Marko was a bright light. We will miss him every day.”

Ana’s mother whispered, “Ana brought beauty into the world. Her spirit will live on.”

The coffins were lowered into the ground as prayers filled the air.

Messages from Heaven

In heaven, two white doves landed beside Abraham.

“The children have been buried,” one dove said softly. “Their families are grieving.”

Abraham wrote two messages:

“Marko is in heaven. He has found peace, tranquility, and happiness.” — Abraham

“Ana is in heaven. She has found peace, tranquility, and happiness.” — Abraham

He tied each message to a dove’s leg.

“Go,” he said. “Bring comfort.”

The doves flew down to Earth, delivering the messages to the grieving families.

Eternal Peace

Marko and Ana continued exploring heaven—discovering waterfalls, hidden groves, and endless fields of flowers. They felt safe, loved, and free.

Abraham watched over them with a tender smile.

“You are remarkable souls,” he said. “Here, you will find true happiness.”

As the sun set in a blaze of colors, Abraham gathered the children and animals.

“In heaven,” he said, “every soul finds its place. You are part of a greater harmony.”

Marko and Ana looked at each other, hearts full of gratitude.

They knew they were home.

Epilogue — The Eternal Guide

In heaven, every child is greeted by Abraham. He becomes their guide, protector, and friend.

War is a terrible thing. But every child lost to war finds a deserved place in heaven—a place of peace, beauty, and love.

Their spirits shine brightly, forever safe, forever cherished.
