Story #10

The Dream of the Old Singer

In a small, impoverished village at the edge of the world lived an old man named Jovan. He had spent his entire life working the land, toiling under harsh sun and unforgiving seasons. The village was simple—stone and wooden houses, hardworking farmers, craftsmen, and laborers bound together by shared hardship.

But Jovan carried something that set him apart: he loved to sing.

Ever since childhood, music had been his refuge. His mother’s lullabies stayed with him throughout his life, and he dreamed of standing on a grand stage, microphone in hand, singing to an adoring audience.

Reality, however, was far from his dreams. The village had no stage, no microphone, no audience. People mocked him, calling him foolish. They joked that his voice was better suited for scaring crows than entertaining anyone.

Yet Jovan never let their ridicule crush his spirit. He sang while walking the dusty streets, while working the fields, while wandering through the forest, and while sitting by the river. Nature became his audience—trees, birds, and flowing water harmonized with his melodies.

In his small, dilapidated house, he kept a makeshift microphone crafted from a wooden stick and a tin can. He would stand before it, pretending to perform for an imaginary crowd. His heart longed for a real microphone, but such a luxury was far beyond his reach.

Mockery followed him daily. Children mimicked his singing, villagers jeered, elders shook their heads. Still, Jovan sang.

One harsh winter, life became even more difficult. The fields were barren, food scarce, and cold winds howled through the cracks of his home. Wrapped in a thin blanket, Jovan continued to sing—his voice a fragile beacon of hope.

Late one snowy night, he sat by a flickering candle, trembling from the cold. Holding his makeshift microphone, he sang softly—each note filled with longing. As the flame danced, his mind drifted into fantasy. He imagined himself on a grand stage, a real microphone in hand, a vast audience cheering for him.

For a moment, he felt hope. Even if his dreams never came true, dreaming gave him strength.

Winter slowly gave way to spring. Snow melted, fields revived, and villagers returned to their work. Jovan found solace in the changing seasons. One evening, he walked through the fields, breathing in fresh air. Birds sang, trees swayed, and Jovan joined them with his own melody. For a moment, dreams and reality blended into harmony.

Spring brought renewed determination. One bright morning, while Jovan worked in his garden, children played nearby. Intrigued by his humming, they gathered around him.

“Sing us a song, Jovan!” one child called.

Jovan smiled and sang a lively folk tune his mother once sang to him. The children clapped and danced, delighted. For the first time in years, Jovan performed for an audience—small, but real. Their joy warmed his heart.

Word spread quickly. Adults paused their work to listen. Mockery faded, replaced by curiosity and appreciation. Jovan sang more often, and villagers began to cherish his gift.

One evening, during a community gathering, Eldric—the village elder—approached him.

“Jovan,” Eldric said warmly, “I underestimated your music. Your singing has brought joy and unity to our village. Thank you.”

Jovan nodded, humbled. He realized he might never stand on a grand stage, but he had found something just as meaningful: the respect and love of his community.

That night, he sat by the river, singing softly under the moonlight. Peace washed over him.

Summer arrived, and the village thrived. Jovan’s songs became part of daily life. One afternoon, a traveling merchant arrived. Among his wares was a beautifully carved music box.

“This box is enchanted,” the merchant said. “Its melodies enchant all who hear them.”

Jovan, captivated, bought it with the few coins he had.

That evening, he wound the music box. A hauntingly beautiful melody filled his home. Jovan sang along, his voice blending with the magical tune. Villagers gathered outside, mesmerized. Even animals paused to listen.

When the melody ended, applause erupted. Jovan stepped outside, overwhelmed by their admiration.

“You must sing more often,” a villager said. “Your music brings joy to us all.”

And so, Jovan’s performances became a cherished part of village life. The music box and his voice created magic that warmed hearts through summer and into autumn.

As winter approached again, Jovan reflected on his journey—from ridicule to appreciation. He wound the music box and sang a song of gratitude, his voice filled with hope and resilience.

One snowy night, as he sang by the fire, Jovan drifted into a dream.

He stood in a vast field surrounded by towering mountains. Before him stood a gigantic vintage microphone glowing with mystical light. Animals gathered around—birds, deer, rabbits. People from distant lands arrived, drawn by his voice.

Jovan sang with all his heart. His voice soared across mountains and valleys. Applause thundered. His lifelong dream came true.

When he awoke, he was back in his small home. Snow fell gently outside. The dream had been magical, fulfilling, and deeply real in his heart.

He looked out at the village, smiling. His dreams had given him strength and purpose. He sang softly, holding the music box—a reminder of the wonder in his life.

Jovan continued living humbly, but with peace. His voice became a beacon of hope and inspiration. He knew he was, and always would be, a true singer.

In the end, Jovan’s dreams wove magic not only into his own life, but into the lives of all who heard his songs. He found his place—not on a grand stage, but in the hearts of his community. And that was more than enough.
