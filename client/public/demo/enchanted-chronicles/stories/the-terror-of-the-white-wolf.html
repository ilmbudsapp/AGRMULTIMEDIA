Story #12

The Terror of the White Wolf

In the heart of a remote, serene valley—nestled between towering mountains and ancient forests—lay the small village of Drakwood. Generations of families had lived there in harmony with nature, tending crops, caring for livestock, and preserving traditions that bound the community together. Evergreen forests whispered ancient secrets, and a majestic snow‑capped mountain stood sentinel to the north.

Life in Drakwood was peaceful—except for one recurring nightmare.

Every year, when the first snow blanketed the ground and long winter nights settled in, a chilling fear gripped the villagers. The fear of the White Wolf—a giant, spectral beast that descended from the mountain once a year to claim a child. Legends said it was a cursed spirit, bound to the mountain and driven by an insatiable hunger. No one knew why it targeted Drakwood, only that its arrival was inevitable.

On a frosty evening, as the sun dipped below the horizon, the villagers gathered in the square. Tension hung heavy in the air.

“It’s almost time,” an old woman whispered, clutching her shawl. “The wolf will come tonight.”

Parents held their children close. Elders tried to offer comfort, but their haunted eyes betrayed their fear.

Darkness fell. An eerie silence settled over Drakwood. Then— A bone‑chilling howl echoed from the mountain.

“It’s coming,” someone breathed.

Panic erupted. Women rushed their children indoors, barricading doors and windows. Men grabbed weapons they knew would be useless. The square emptied as everyone hid, praying for safety.

Through thick fog, a massive ghostly figure appeared. The White Wolf—towering, majestic, and terrifying—descended with ethereal grace. Its fur glowed white as fresh snow, its eyes burned with otherworldly light, and its paws left no trace.

It moved silently through the village, scanning homes. It stopped at a modest cottage where a young girl with golden braids stood frozen in fear. Before anyone could react, the wolf lunged, gently but firmly grasped the child, and vanished into the forest. Her cries faded into the night.

Drakwood fell into stunned silence. Another child lost.

The villagers knew they needed a hero.

Rumors spoke of Zindijan—a fearless warrior of unmatched courage. The elders sent a delegation to find him. When they described their plight, Zindijan agreed to help.

Upon arriving in Drakwood, he was welcomed with desperate hope. He spent weeks studying the wolf’s patterns, listening to elders’ stories, and preparing for the inevitable confrontation.

Finally, the night arrived.

A familiar howl echoed through the village. Panic spread. Zindijan gripped his sword, ready.

Through the fog, the White Wolf emerged—glowing eyes fixed on the village. Zindijan stepped forward.

The beast lunged. Zindijan deflected the attack, striking back with fierce precision. The battle was intense—a dance of strength and agility. The wolf, sensing danger, suddenly bolted into the forest. Zindijan chased it into the labyrinth of shadows.

Branches whipped his face, uneven ground threatened to trip him, but he pressed on. The wolf moved with supernatural speed. Zindijan’s heart pounded, muscles burned, but he refused to give up.

The wolf vanished into the undergrowth. Zindijan halted, scanning the darkness. Silence. He knew the beast was close.

Without warning, the wolf sprang from the shadows. Its claws raked across his chest. Pain seared through him, but he fought back, driving the wolf away.

The battle resumed—swift, brutal, relentless. Blood stained Zindijan’s tunic. The wolf struck again, tearing into his arm. He cried out but held his sword firm.

With a roar of defiance, Zindijan struck the wolf across the muzzle. It staggered, blood dripping from its snout. The wolf circled him—rage and respect in its eyes.

The final clash was brutal. The wolf lunged. Zindijan met it head‑on. With a final surge of strength, he drove his sword deep into the beast’s side.

The wolf howled in agony, then collapsed. The forest fell silent.

Zindijan dropped to his knees, vision blurring. But he had done it. Drakwood was safe.

He broke off one of the wolf’s enormous fangs—proof of victory—and began the agonizing journey back. Hours later, at dawn, he emerged from the forest. The villagers waited in the square, hearts heavy with hope.

When they saw him—bloodied, exhausted, but alive—they gasped. Zindijan raised the massive fang. The villagers erupted in cheers. Mothers wept. Fathers embraced him. Children stared in awe.

The elder stepped forward.

“Today we honor Zindijan, who freed us from the terror of the White Wolf. Let this day be remembered forever.”

A feast followed. Laughter returned to Drakwood. Children played freely again.

The elders proposed renaming the central square in Zindijan’s honor.

“Zindijan Square!” the villagers chanted.

The elder placed a hand on Zindijan’s shoulder. “From this day forward, this square will bear your name. You have given us hope and a future free from fear.”

As the sun set over Drakwood, casting golden light over Zindijan Square, the villagers knew their future was bright. They had overcome their greatest fear. Zindijan’s name would live on—a symbol of courage and resilience for generations.
